I. Why conventional supervised training is currently inappropriate:
   Most overlapping Raags (like Bairagi, Multani, Marwa) only have a single recording in SARAGA.
   A supervised classifier trained on one recording will perfectly memorize the singer's voice, tempo, and instruments, but completely fail to generalize to a different artist or genre (like the Folk dataset). There is zero intra-class variance.

J. Why segment-level splitting would cause leakage:
   Taking a single 15-minute recording, cutting it into 5-second segments, and randomly dividing them into Train/Validation/Test sets causes massive data leakage. The model will see segments from the exact same singer/recording session in both training and testing, artificially inflating accuracy without learning true musical invariants.

K. Important limitations of using SARAGA as a reference bank:
   SARAGA contains high-quality long-form classical performances, but lacks the dataset diversity needed for robust Raag reference. A single recording may focus heavily on a specific movement (e.g., Alap) and not capture the full melodic progression of the Raag. Comparing lively, rhythmic 2-minute Folk songs to a single 20-minute slow classical Alap using basic acoustic features could yield poor similarity scores due to tempo and stylistic mismatch rather than Raag mismatch.
